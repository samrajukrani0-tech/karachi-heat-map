Karachi Heat Priority Map

A free map that suggests where heat-relief support should go first in Landhi Town during a heatwave, and says why. Support means drinking water, ORS, cooling points and ambulance standby. It is a tool for your team's judgement, not a replacement for it.

What it is

  • Landhi is split into 265 small areas of about 0.1 km². Each is ranked from how hot the ground gets on summer afternoons (satellite data), how many people live there, and how little shade and access to a clinic it has.
  • Tap any area to see why it ranks where it does, and how sure the model is.
  • Plan supplies: say where your stock is and how much you have, and it suggests how to split it, highest priority first, within the distance your team can reach.
  • Field briefs: one printable page per named place, small enough for WhatsApp.
  • Works on a phone, and offline once it has been opened once.

What it is not

  • Not an official warning system. For heatwave warnings, follow the Pakistan Meteorological Department and PDMA Sindh.
  • Not a prediction of deaths, and never a verdict on any neighbourhood.
  • Not finished. The weights are placeholders and no relief centre has been checked in person yet. The population data counts about half the people the 2023 census found. It cannot see power cuts, which the Edhi Foundation named as a major factor in June 2024.

How to read the map in two minutes

  1. Open the link below. Darker areas are higher priority for support within Landhi. It is a ranking, not a measurement.
  2. Tap an area. You see its rank, the reasons in plain words, and its values.
  3. Dashed outlines are areas the model is unsure about. Your own knowledge matters most there.
  4. Plan supplies gives a quick estimate of how to split your stock. Treat it as a starting point.

What would help most

  1. Where is it wrong? Name places it ranks too high or too low, and why.
  2. Rank a dozen places yourself, before looking at the map, on the one-page form. This is how we find out whether the map is any use.
  3. Your centre: where it is, whether it can hold stock, how far you deliver, and how much water per person you plan for.
  4. Power cuts: which areas are worst. The feeder name printed on an electricity bill would let us add power cuts to the map honestly.
